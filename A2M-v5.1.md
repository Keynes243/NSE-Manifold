## Abstract

This monograph proposes the **Activity Frontier Model**. A continuously active system already occupies a state of its large ontology before any new input arrives. Input first changes that state’s judgment of the current situation; it does not receive consciousness-level causal authority merely by entering through an external channel. The future permitted by the large ontology is not a single prewritten trajectory. At any moment, it consists of a bundle of near-term continuations that remain live. This monograph calls that bundle the **path fan**, and calls the low-capacity, high-gain working surface formed by the large ontology at the fan’s current branching point the **activity frontier**. The activity frontier is the concrete candidate offered here for a dynamic context or consciousness interface.

The frontier is defined not by which words it contains, but by which influences currently possess enough authority to alter the next transition. Its purpose is not to carry a miniature copy of the large ontology. It carries the influences required to preserve the current feasibility boundary and to distinguish among the continuations still alive within that boundary. An item may remain in the large ontology indefinitely without entering the frontier. Another item may be irrelevant at task onset and acquire frontier status only after activity exposes a later branch on which it becomes decisive. The frontier must therefore be formed again after every consequential step: the path fan, the unresolved distinctions, and the historical influences capable of redirecting the system have all changed.

In this monograph, **regeneration** is a causal property, not a textual one. Identical content may persist across adjacent steps and still count as regenerated if the new state independently renews its authority. Conversely, wording may be rewritten at every step while the same influence is copied forward without review; that is still static continuation. This distinction prevents dynamic context from collapsing into periodic summarization.

To let history enter during activity rather than only at its conclusion, the model introduces the **causal trace**. A past trajectory and its consequences need not survive as a complete retrievable record. They may instead leave a directed deformation in the transition structure of the large ontology. As a current local trajectory develops a similar causal organization, that deformation begins to alter the activity frontier: it may slow commitment, reopen a distinction that was being compressed away, or increase the reachability of an alternative path. A trace capable of transfer without indiscriminate false alarms must functionally preserve at least three things together: what structure constitutes entry into the old path, in what direction the previous correction redirected activity, and which decisive difference should release the current case from that correction. These are termed the **entry structure**, **directional correction**, and **release condition**.

The two constructs form a single loop. Persistent state internalizes input; the current path fan forms; unresolved distinctions generate an activity frontier; the frontier advances activity by one step; the new step exposes differences that the old frontier did not carry; the growing local trajectory couples to a causal trace; historical influence enters and reorganizes the frontier; and the redirected result writes back into persistent state, changing how the next frontier will form. Déjà vu is therefore not a feature appended to dynamic context. It is a particular regeneration of the frontier after historical influence acquires causal force.

Most of this monograph is spent showing how this candidate runs, how it handles one repeated error before the second error is committed, and how it avoids equating familiarity with identity. Formalism is restricted to a minimal translation, and experimentation to four load-bearing attacks. The central open problem is how a large ontology could learn suitable influence patterns and causal traces. This monograph specifies the functional structure they would need to acquire; it does not supply a training algorithm.

## 1. Constructive Priority: Build the Trunk Before Measuring the Branches

Three requirements motivate the construction developed here: context should not be generated only once at task onset; the eventual availability of a past influence does not imply that it participated in time; and the current local trajectory may become the key that reopens an older one. These requirements identify a problem, but they do not yet specify how the process occurs.

A compact functional notation might represent the dependency as

$$
C_t=\mathcal G(B_t,R_t)
$$

This expression says that a large ontology generates context from its current state and the results of historical access. The causal order is plausible, but the notation leaves the decisive questions unanswered. What change inside $B_t$ makes an influence newly eligible for $C_t$? Which old influences should remain? Which ones remain true but should lose their position in the high-gain interface? How does a stored correction become an influence that is changing the next step now?

This monograph does not answer those gaps by adding more placeholder functions. It proposes a candidate construction that can be followed from beginning to end. The construction is not presumed correct. Its immediate objective is to achieve enough internal articulation that a failed component can be identified and replaced precisely, instead of surrounding an unspecified mechanism with additional evaluation criteria.

The central claim can be compressed into one sentence:

> **Context is the activity frontier formed by a persistent large ontology where the current path can still change; causal traces left by history alter that frontier when the developing local trajectory becomes capable of coupling to them.**

Why static context is insufficient, whether an influence arrived on time, and how structural transfer should be tested all remain important. Here, however, they are branches. The trunk is the account of how the frontier forms, moves, and is altered by an old path.

## 2. The Activity Frontier: Consciousness as a Working Surface at the Current Branch

### 2.1 What “frontier” means

At every moment, the large ontology is richer than current consciousness. It contains long-formed dispositions, newly internalized input, ongoing tasks, functions not presently invoked, effects left by prior experience, and the system’s current load, conflict, and stability conditions. Copying all of this into context would destroy the point of a consciousness interface. Selecting a few fragments by surface relevance cannot explain why a historical influence that was useless at the beginning becomes indispensable halfway through a task.

The Activity Frontier Model changes the cross-section through which the problem is viewed. Current activity does not decide the whole future at once. At a local position, it takes one actual step from among several continuations that remain possible. The locus at which the next transition can still be changed is the **activity frontier**.

The frontier has four defining properties:

1. **It is local.** It serves the continuations still alive over the next step or short horizon; it does not attempt to represent the entire large ontology.
2. **It is high-gain.** Once an influence enters the frontier, it can materially alter the next transition rather than remaining low-weight background.
3. **It moves.** Each realized step closes some branches and opens others; the frontier moves with that change.
4. **It is defined by effect.** Words, vectors, activation patterns, temporary weights, or distributed states may all carry frontier influence. Their representational form does not decide whether they constitute context.

The frontier need not be a separately encoded physical module, nor is it a small object suspended outside the large ontology. It is a functional cross-section of the same continuous activity under the question: *what strongly governs the next step now?* As a projection of the large ontology, it is part of the activity; as a cause of the next transition, it is a set of high-gain influences. Both descriptions hold simultaneously.

### 2.2 The path fan: the frontier faces live continuations, not an information store

Suppose the current large-ontology state permits several near-term continuations. The model does not require the system to enumerate its entire future. It calls the local continuations not yet excluded by the current state—and still capable of being realized by the next transition—the **path fan**.

A path fan can be ordinary. The next step in a proof may be to continue the present derivation, recheck a premise, recruit an older theorem, defer commitment, or change representation. In a multi-turn interaction, the next step may be to accept a new input, retain the prior judgment, detect a contradiction with an earlier constraint, or reinterpret the situation. The fan may also be continuous; it need not arrive already divided into a finite list of routes.

The frontier does not preserve the complete content of every path. It preferentially carries two classes of function. One maintains the feasibility boundary of the present fan so that routes already closed by facts, goals, or constraints do not silently re-enter. The other keeps the paths still inside that boundary sufficiently distinct from one another.

- If a constraint applies to every currently feasible path but removing it would reopen an unacceptable path, an equivalent form of that constraint may still require high-gain support.
- If a common influence is already stably carried by the large-ontology background, and withdrawing it from the frontier changes neither the boundary nor any live-path distinction, it need not occupy the frontier at that moment.
- If an unresolved distinction would send two live paths in opposite directions on the next step, it deserves high frontier priority.
- If an old experience becomes path-altering only after a later structure appears, its absence from the opening frontier is desirable rather than defective.
- If an influence is semantically related to the topic but would change no live continuation, it may remain in the background.

The selection principle is therefore narrower and more action-bearing than “retrieve the most relevant information”:

> **Preferentially grant high gain to influences that preserve the current feasibility boundary or produce a decisive distinction among continuations still alive within it.**

This is a theoretical candidate, not a universal implementation rule already confirmed by the user. Its value lies in converting “necessary information” from a retrospective list into a relation of effect at a current branch. Necessity still depends on goal, risk, cost, and horizon, but it no longer requires the system to know the final solution at task onset.

### 2.3 Influence patterns: the frontier carries effects, not necessarily content items

For expository purposes, a relatively stable mode of local causal action available within the large ontology will be called an **influence pattern**. An influence pattern may be an explicit rule, but it may also be:

- suppression or amplification of a class of candidate paths;
- an unresolved conflict;
- a direction of search;
- a coarse internal sense of current capability, progress, or risk;
- a directional tendency left by a past trajectory;
- an integrated effect that cannot be decomposed into independent information items.

“Pattern” is an analytical unit, not a claim that the substrate stores a literal table of patterns. Nor does the model force state into separate content and bias compartments. The term is used to prevent context from reverting to “a set of retrievable text fragments.” The frontier must carry effects capable of altering a local trajectory; content is only one possible carrier of such effects.

The activity frontier can be understood as a small set of influence patterns that currently possess high gain. It is not obtained by placing the top-scoring items into a window. Influences can complement, cancel, compete, or combine into a new whole; distinct contents can also exert the same local function. The frontier aims to carry the distinctions that matter to current paths, not to display content identities faithfully.

### 2.4 A small frontier does not imply that the large ontology must first become a summary

Limited frontier capacity is one condition of clear causal action. If every background influence received consciousness-level authority at once, the system would be flooded by irrelevant constraints, incompatible directions, and historical residue. Yet a capacity limit does not require the large ontology to be read in full and converted into an optimal summary before activity can continue.

The relation proposed here is different. The current state of the large ontology already has a nonuniform accessibility structure. Task, goal, recent activity, internal condition, and historical effects jointly determine which patterns are presently easy to activate. The frontier forms from a local region already shaped by state, and current activity in turn reshapes what is accessible. Search is not a full scan over a static warehouse; it unfolds through regions opened by the evolving state.

This also explains why the right history is sometimes not merely “ranked too low.” At task onset it may not belong to the candidate region at all. Only after the trajectory changes the state does that influence gain a route into activity. The central property of dynamic context is therefore not repeated search over a fixed candidate pool. Activity itself changes what can become a candidate next.

## 3. What Regeneration Strictly Means: Every Step Must Renew Causal Authority

“Context is repeatedly regenerated” easily degenerates into a cosmetic operation: every few steps, summarize the transcript, vary the wording, and append the new summary. Such a system may appear continuously updated while allowing inherited content to renew itself indefinitely through inertia.

The model defines regeneration through **reauthorization**.

Assume an influence $i$ belongs to the activity frontier at time $t$. After activity advances to $t+1$, its continued frontier status cannot be justified solely by the fact that it was present one moment earlier. Its authority must be renewed by the new large-ontology state, the new path fan, and the unresolved distinctions now at stake. Prior presence may create a continuity bias; it cannot provide a permanent visa.

Four cases follow:

| Surface behavior | Causal situation | Genuine regeneration? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The same content persists | The new state still needs it to preserve a boundary or distinguish a decisive branch, so high gain is renewed | Yes |
| The same content persists | It is copied mechanically and would remain even after the task changed | No |
| Content is rephrased | Its actual authority is inherited without review; only its surface form changes | No |
| The original content disappears and another state carries an equivalent constraint | The new state reconstructs the required effect in another form | Yes |

Continuity of the frontier is therefore causal relay, not document copying. The relation mirrors the more general claim that a persistent influence survives by being re-instantiated from moment to moment. The old frontier acts directly only on the next transition. The resulting state then determines which influences continue the relay. No context object exerts force unchanged across the entire activity.

Reauthorization does not mean starting from zero after every step. Discarding the previous state completely would create oscillation and excessive cost. A more plausible candidate dynamics would:

- preserve continuity for influences still governing the current branch;
- reduce the gain of influences whose distinctions have been resolved, whose role has disappeared, or whose basis has been overturned;
- open routes for influences made newly valuable by the latest transition;
- reorganize an effect into a cheaper equivalent representation when possible;
- allow an unresolved distinction itself to occupy the frontier when premature commitment could create irreversible error.

These are not five checks that must be executed sequentially. They are effects the frontier dynamics should produce. Design supplies the niche: a high-gain surface that governs the next transition and remains continuously shaped by current state. The concrete mechanisms of persistence, competition, decay, and reorganization should emerge through training in the activity as a whole.

## 4. One Complete Cycle: From Input to Redirection

The candidate mechanism can now be stated without equations. The seven stages below are analytical cuts, not a requirement for seven serial engineering modules. A substrate may implement the same causal relations in parallel, continuously, or distributively.

### 4.1 Stage One: the large ontology is already active

Before input arrives, the system is not blank. It is already operating with previously formed goals, task state, long-term dispositions, available functions, historical effects, and internal conditions. Activity need not fall to zero in the absence of an external message. The system may maintain stability, integrate slowly, anticipate likely input, or simply prevent relevant state from dispersing into noise.

This matters because every later act of “generation” depends on more than input. Identical inputs falling on different large-ontology states may form different activity frontiers; different inputs that bring the ontology into functionally equivalent states may form similar frontiers.

### 4.2 Stage Two: input is internalized rather than written directly into consciousness

On arrival, input first changes the large ontology’s judgment of the current situation. That change may be large or small; the input may be recognized as repetition, noise, or an unresolved contradiction. Once transformed, source identity need not permanently determine its treatment, although source, credibility, and relation to existing state may matter while internalization is still occurring.

The frontier need not contain the input verbatim. Input may function as a key: it can make an older constraint, long-term goal, or previously inaccessible history newly capable of distinguishing the next step. What receives consciousness-level authority is the configuration of influence formed by the large ontology after internalization.

### 4.3 Stage Three: the current path fan takes shape

As situational judgment changes, a bundle of feasible near-term continuations appears. The model does not require the system to generate every path explicitly and then score them. The path fan may exist as implicit competition, local sampling, a continuous vector field, or another structure. What matters is that more than one continuation remains practically possible and that some differences among them can alter the next step.

The system’s immediate problem is no longer “what in the whole knowledge base is relevant to this input?” It is: “what currently distinguishes the continuations still alive?” The question contracts global search to the current locus of activity.

### 4.4 Stage Four: the activity frontier forms

Influence patterns capable of materially changing the current path fan acquire high gain and combine into the activity frontier. Background material whose work is already complete remains in the large ontology. Historical influences not yet at a decisive stage may also remain latent.

A viable frontier must perform at least three functions, although they need not occupy three physical slots:

- **Maintain constraints that must remain effective now.** Otherwise the system would forget the task after every step.
- **Expose unresolved differences that currently separate live paths.** Otherwise the system would treat an open question as already decided.
- **Leave an entry point for influences capable of forcing redirection.** Otherwise the frontier becomes a self-sealing commitment rather than a moving working surface.

Together these functions prevent the frontier from becoming either a pure “current answer” or an unfiltered heap of background. It behaves more like a short-lived set of parameters governing local transition rules.

### 4.5 Stage Five: the frontier advances activity by one step

Under the frontier’s high-gain influences, the system completes an internal transition: it forms an intermediate judgment, excludes a route, raises a subproblem, recruits a function, or approaches an output commitment. The result writes back into the large ontology as the new actual state.

The original path fan necessarily changes. Some routes are closed by what the system has now done. Previously invisible distinctions may appear. A constraint that was sufficient one step ago may no longer suffice. A historical influence may become accessible for the first time because the local trajectory has acquired new structure.

### 4.6 Stage Six: unresolved pressure and causal traces begin to act

Between the new state and the old frontier there may appear an **unresolved pressure**: the old frontier was sufficient to reach the present point, but no longer sufficient to keep the next step well-directed. This pressure may appear as prediction error, constraint conflict, anomalous familiarity, stalled search, abrupt risk increase, or the realization that a previously absent distinction now determines the outcome.

The pressure does not mean that the system already knows which missing fact would produce the correct answer. It indicates only that the current working surface can no longer maintain adequate distinction among the newly exposed continuations. It reshapes accessibility inside the large ontology, making influences coupled to this form of mismatch, conflict, or local trajectory more likely to enter candidacy.

If an earlier trajectory and its correction left a causal trace with a similar structure, the developing local trajectory may now couple to it. The first result need not be full recollection. It may instead be a sudden loss of confidence in the current route, the reopening of a compressed-away distinction, delayed commitment, or the admission of another function into candidacy. Historical influence is already active if it changes current activity in any of these ways.

### 4.7 Stage Seven: the frontier reorganizes and the redirected result writes back

Newly admitted influences compete and combine with still-valid prior influences to form the next activity frontier. Some content persists because the new state needs it again; some is withdrawn; some is replaced by an equivalent effect in another form. If a causal trace emits only a coarse warning, the frontier may first turn toward verification. If further unfolding confirms that the old correction applies, the directional correction gains authority. If a decisive difference activates a release condition, the system drops the false analogy and resumes the current route.

The redirected result updates the large ontology again. If the old error was successfully avoided, the result is more than an output. It modifies future accessibility around similar trajectories, strengthening, correcting, or adding a release condition to the relevant causal trace. Growth thereby closes the loop: processing an input changes how a later input will be processed.

Only the full seven-stage cycle deserves the name “dynamic context regeneration” here. If changes in the path fan, unresolved pressure, and historical entry are removed, leaving only “state generates context; context updates state,” the model remains empty. If each transition is replaced with a fixed checklist, it falls back into workflow. The activity frontier is intended to occupy the space between: an explicit functional dynamics that does not pre-script every action of the organ that realizes it.

## 5. Causal Traces: How the Past Alters the Present Frontier Without Returning as Complete Memory

### 5.1 A trace is not a stored copy of the old path

The most obvious way to preserve a useful past trajectory is to store it in full and retrieve it when a similar input appears. That strategy can be entirely adequate in some settings. It is not, however, the minimum condition for the function required here. The concrete content of an old event may disappear while the chain of effects it initiated continues to alter later state. What must persist is the way the past changed the accessibility structure of future activity.

This change is called a **causal trace**. A trace is not a copy of history. It is a directed deformation imposed by an earlier trajectory and its consequences on the transition structure of the large ontology. If a path once carried the system into error and was subsequently corrected, the large ontology should not remain perfectly symmetric when it later approaches a similar causal organization. Direct commitment to the old route may become harder; a distinction absent the first time may enter the frontier more easily; an alternative search route may open earlier.

“Trace” is a functional term. The deformation may be carried by long-term parameter change, persistent state, external memory, distributed constraint, a modified attractor landscape, or another substrate. The model requires only that the trace can re-exert directional influence during current activity. It does not require the system to locate a record labelled “previous error.”

This is the decisive difference from ordinary similarity retrieval. Similarity retrieval first returns content; a causal trace first changes the **manner of proceeding**. A system may avoid one additional wrong step before it can report what happened previously. Detailed history can still be reconstructed or accessed when useful, but such recovery is a second stage rather than the precondition for historical influence.

### 5.2 The minimum functional structure of a usable trace

Retaining only “this path is dangerous” is insufficient. It collapses every local resemblance into the same warning. The resulting mechanism either fires everywhere or becomes so costly that the system learns to ignore it. A trace that transfers, redirects, and withdraws when a decisive difference appears must functionally preserve at least three coupled components.

#### I. Entry structure: what is carrying current activity into the old path?

The entry structure is not the topic or wording of the old task. It is the causal relation through which the old error began to form. Examples include:

- compressing variables that belong to different levels into one;
- inferring “must be selected” directly from “can enter candidacy”;
- treating one local correlation as a stable cause;
- committing before a decisive condition has appeared;
- allowing repetition from an external source to substitute for new evidence and gradually acquire greater authority.

The entry structure may consist only of an incomplete prefix. A shorter prefix covers more old paths but produces more false alarms. A longer prefix is more discriminating but may arrive after the point at which redirection remains possible. A well-formed trace does not wait for complete recurrence. Its influence increases as the balance shifts between adequate discrimination and remaining time to intervene.

#### II. Directional correction: which relation did the previous correction actually change?

After an error is identified, storing the sentence “that was wrong” does little. What matters is what the correction did to the structure of activity. It may have:

- separated two levels that had been compressed;
- returned an omitted variable to candidacy;
- reversed a causal edge;
- deferred commitment until a decisive condition appeared;
- reduced an input’s authority while preserving its value as a clue.

A directional correction need not contain the answer to the current problem. It behaves more like a force that changes search priority and frontier composition: first make the system ask the right question again, then allow present state to determine the answer. Only in this form can one trace transfer across content rather than impose an old answer on a new task.

#### III. Release condition: which decisive difference means that this is not the old path?

Structural transfer must recognize difference as well as similarity. The release condition records which relations were essential to the old error and therefore which change should terminate the old correction’s control over the current frontier.

For example, a trace expressing “equal access to candidacy does not imply equal selection frequency” should release when a present rule explicitly requires rotation, quotas, or equal long-run frequency. In that regime, moving from equal accessibility to equal frequency may no longer be erroneous because the institution itself binds the two levels together. Without a release condition, a distinction learned from experience becomes a new dogma.

Entry structure, directional correction, and release condition need not be three literal fields in the substrate. They are the minimum functional decomposition of a trace that is meant to transfer without permanent capture. A trace may also carry consequence severity, confidence, age, and access cost, but these properties presuppose the three-part core.

### 5.3 How traces form: error, consequence, and correction must join the same chain

One correction does not automatically create a useful causal trace. If the system stores only the user’s final correct statement, it may reproduce the conclusion later while remaining unable to recognize that its current reasoning is generating the old error again. Trace formation requires four periods to become causally connected:

1. which local relations pushed activity toward the erroneous route;
2. at what step the error changed from a reversible hypothesis into a premise for later reasoning;
3. which relation was identified as defective by the external consequence or correction;
4. how the trajectory changed after correction, and why the new result was preferable.

The system need not produce a fixed-format postmortem. Training may use outcome differences to couple the local structure that generated the error to the correction that successfully redirected it. External teaching is not a designer hard-coding a workflow. It supplies high-value input to an existing organ, allowing the activity as a whole to grow a new connection along a tractable path.

Error plus punishment without an effective correction tends to produce broad avoidance. A correct answer without the causal chain that produced the old error tends to produce local imitation. Correction without counterexamples and release conditions tends to produce unlimited extrapolation. The quality of a causal trace depends on whether these effects genuinely connect within one loop of growth.

### 5.4 How a present trajectory activates a trace: the local path becomes a gradually forming key

At task onset, the current trajectory is short and many distinct futures share the same prefix. Pulling every remotely compatible trace into the frontier would create noise. As activity proceeds, however, the local trajectory acquires not only content but causal shape: which distinctions the system used, which variable it omitted, how it moved from one judgment to another, and where it is about to commit.

That growing structure changes accessibility inside the large ontology. A coupled causal trace may move from negligible effect, to a mild bias on search, to enough force to reorganize the frontier. The current local trajectory is therefore a key, but not because it is submitted as an exact query against the whole archive. It is a key because it carries the system into a state from which an old influence can act again.

Trace influence may unfold in grades:

- **Orientation.** Only part of the entry structure is present. The trace slightly reduces unconditional trust in the current route, broadens candidacy, or delays commitment.
- **Verification.** Decisive structure continues to appear. The trace brings the correction’s central distinction into the frontier while actively seeking a release condition.
- **Expansion.** More of the old case is reconstructed only if concrete history would improve discrimination, explain a present difference, or calibrate risk.
- **Exit.** A decisive edge differs from the old path; the release condition lowers the trace’s authority and prevents historical capture.

Déjà vu is consequently not a binary switch. It can first appear as a vague, low-cost directional influence and then strengthen, become more specific, or dissolve as evidence develops. The correct moment is not simply “as early as possible.” Too early, the prefix is nondiscriminating; too late, the commitment deadline has already passed. Trace influence must become strong enough to change the frontier between those limits.

### 5.5 A causal trace is not automatically correct

A causal trace is one way for the past to affect the present, not a truth channel. The earlier correction may itself have been wrong; the environment may have changed; the entry structure may be learned too broadly; the release condition may be too narrow. A trace that once protected the system may become prejudice under a new regime.

Traces must therefore remain inside the same growth loop. Each activation and its current result should alter the trace in return. Successful redirection may strengthen the connection between entry and correction. A false alarm may add a release condition. A miss may extend the trace to an earlier prefix. Environmental change may reduce the authority of the old correction. Forgetting need not require a separate deletion mechanism: a trace that persistently fails to coordinate with current activity and produces no useful redirection can gradually lose effect through competition and reauthorization.

## 6. Worked Example: Recognizing “Two Levels Were Collapsed Again” Before Repeating the Error

The following example does not establish the mechanism. It places the activity frontier and causal trace inside one observable run. It deliberately uses two tasks with different surface domains but the same error-generating structure.

### 6.1 The earlier error

The earlier task concerned a design principle: when different functions are appropriate, the underlying difficulty of bringing each into the decision’s candidate set should be as even as possible. Which function is actually invoked should then be determined by the situation.

The system produced this erroneous chain:

1. It read “treat invocation difficulty equally.”
2. It compressed **accessibility to candidacy** and **post-decision invocation frequency** into one level.
3. It inferred that every function would have to be invoked at the same frequency.
4. Because equal-frequency invocation was plainly undesirable, it rejected the original design principle.

The correction was that equality constrains low-level invocability, while actual frequency remains a result of decision. The failure did not consist in ignorance of the sentence “a decision mechanism exists.” The decision-before and decision-after levels were flattened inside the reasoning chain.

If the episode forms a useful causal trace, its functional structure is:

- **Entry structure:** infer equal outcome frequency directly from equal opportunity or accessibility, bypassing an intermediate decision.
- **Directional correction:** reopen the distinction between entering candidacy and being selected from among candidates; inspect the decision layer between them.
- **Release condition:** if the current institution explicitly mandates averaging, rotation, or fixed quotas at the outcome level, the two stages are genuinely bound and the old correction should no longer block criticism.

The trace does not need to preserve the earlier task’s full wording. The old experience continues to shape activity if these three functions can be relayed into a later frontier.

### 6.2 A new task with entirely different surface content

The new task concerns investigative method. A research system is intended to let statistical tests, archival retrieval, interviews, and mechanism simulation enter the hypothesis pool smoothly whenever each is applicable. Which method is ultimately used is determined by evidence, cost, and risk.

A critic says:

> “If every method can enter the hypothesis pool equally easily, must the system not use every method equally often?”

The surface domain has changed from function invocation to investigative method, but the error structure is the same. The run can be unfolded through the activity frontier:

| Stage | Large ontology and current path fan | High-gain frontier influence | Causal-trace action | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Input internalization | Two preliminary routes form: accept the criticism, or distinguish admission from selection | “Equal entry to the hypothesis pool”; “actual use depends on evidence” | The local trajectory is still too short; the trace remains weakly accessible | The system begins analyzing the criticism |
| First transition | “Equally easy to enter” starts becoming “equally likely to be used” | Equality, frequency, and efficiency cost | The first part of the entry structure appears: accessibility is sliding toward outcome frequency | Confidence in the route declines slightly, but it is not yet rejected |
| Second transition | The system is about to reject the principle because equal use would waste resources | “If frequency is equal, resources are wasted” dominates the frontier | The entry structure is nearly complete; the trace introduces a new distinction: “Does equality apply before or after decision?” | The pending rejection is suspended |
| Frontier reorganization | The path fan reopens: either the institution requires equal use, or it requires only equal accessibility followed by selective use | “Candidate-admission layer / final-selection layer” becomes the central temporary parameter | The directional correction raises the accessibility of a two-stage model | The two levels are no longer compressed into one variable |
| Release check | The text explicitly states that evidence, cost, and risk determine final use | The intermediate decision layer is confirmed | The release condition does not fire; the old correction applies | The critic’s inference is judged invalid |
| Pre-output transition | The response is organized while preserving the conditional branch | “Equal accessibility does not imply equal use; a separate quota rule would be different” | The trace has completed its function; the old case need not be reconstructed | The second error is prevented before commitment |

## 7. How the Activity Frontier Differs from Familiar Alternatives

Retrieval, summarization, working memory, recurrent networks, and external tools may all be used to implement an activity frontier. The frontier is nevertheless not identical to any of them. The distinction lies in the operating relation, not the label.

| Scheme | Core operation | When information enters the high-gain region | How old content persists | Decisive difference from the activity frontier |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One-shot initial retrieval | Retrieve material from the initial input | At task onset | Fixed use until task completion | A structure revealed only later cannot alter the candidate region |
| Append-only context | Add each new input and intermediate result to the end | Whenever new content appears | Preserved by default until truncation | Frequency of appearance approximates consciousness-level authority; no stepwise reauthorization |
| Rolling summary | Periodically compress existing text | At the summary schedule | Copied forward in shorter form | Surface dynamism does not imply that the current branch reviewed the causal influence |
| Ordinary similarity retrieval | Match a current query to historical content | When the query resembles stored content | Return an old item | Content is returned before conduct changes; cross-surface structural transfer is weak |
| Fixed self-checklist | Execute predetermined questions at each step | At a fixed workflow node | Designer-installed rules persist permanently | Maintenance remains at the workflow level; distinctions cannot form from the new situation itself |
| Activity frontier | Form a high-gain working surface around differences among live paths in the current large ontology | At any internal step when a new distinction or trace begins to alter the trajectory | An influence relays forward only if the new state renews its authority | Context identity is defined by actual path effect and reauthorization; history may change conduct before deciding whether to restore content |

This comparison does not claim that the alternatives are useless. Initial retrieval may supply opening candidates, rolling summaries may reduce carrier cost, and fixed checklists may provide engineering baselines. The claim is only that none of them alone supplies the dynamic consciousness interface required here. They occupy the activity-frontier niche only when incorporated into a persistent-state–frontier–state loop in which current paths repeatedly reauthorize the influences that actually govern activity.

### 7.1 Why this is not merely a new name for attention

An ordinary attention mechanism may compute weights between a current query and a set of keys. The activity frontier adds three relations:

1. the query is shaped jointly by the persistent large ontology and the local trajectory already unfolding; it is not identical to the latest input;
2. the reachable candidate region changes as activity advances, so the correct influence may previously have been outside the pool;
3. high-weight influence must regain eligibility from the new state at every step rather than renew itself automatically through historical position.

If an attention system actually implements these three relations, it can serve as the substrate of an activity frontier. No terminological separation is then required. The niche is defined by effect.

### 7.2 Why this is not merely “smarter memory”

The activity frontier is not organized around memory. Current goals, internal load, new input, logical constraints, unresolved conflict, and historical traces may all become frontier influences. Memory is only one family of ways in which the past continues to alter the present.

Conversely, a causal trace need not enter reportable consciousness. If it changes how the frontier forms, it is already performing a memory function. “Memory is usable” thereby separates from “the system can recount an old episode.” A system may be unable to narrate the past case yet avoid the old error in time; it may also recount the case perfectly but fail again because the recollection arrives after commitment.

## 8. Growth and Multi-Turn Dynamics: Design the Frontier Niche, Let Conduct Grow Through Training

### 8.1 What design supplies and what training must grow

This project follows a design route while excluding fixed intelligent workflows. For the activity frontier, design must supply at least the following topological conditions:

- a large-ontology state richer than the consciousness interface and continuously active between inputs;
- an interface through which external input first meets the large ontology rather than receiving direct frontier write access;
- a capacity-limited activity frontier with high gain over the next transition;
- a frontier whose composition can change with internal activity even when no new external input arrives;
- a current local trajectory capable of altering the accessibility of historical influences and functions;
- writeback from frontier-mediated activity into the large ontology, so that processing changes future processing.

Design need not install rules such as “check levels whenever a level confusion appears” or “retrieve history whenever something feels familiar.” Training must instead grow the couplings: which influence patterns become mutually accessible in which states; what form of unresolved pressure opens which candidate region; when a causal trace should emit only a weak directional signal, when it should redirect forcefully, and when it should release.

A runtime controller could impose these behaviors from outside and still produce a useful system, but it would not realize the organ-level symbiosis proposed here. The activity frontier should belong to the system during training. Long-term structure learns to generate short-lived, high-gain structure; the consequences of that short-lived structure continue to reshape long-term state.

### 8.2 Where multi-turn erosion belongs in this model

Separating input from the frontier does not make input powerless. Input must still update the large ontology; otherwise the system could neither learn, accept correction, nor adapt to new fact. The difference is that occurrence through the input channel does not automatically confer consciousness-level authority.

In multi-turn induction, each input may alter state only slightly while dozens of repetitions push the large ontology into a different region of accessibility. The frontier must respond not merely to whether one message crosses a threshold, but to whether the trajectory of persistent-state change is reproducing a causal pattern that previously generated distortion. Here, “the current local trajectory as key” extends across turns. The key is not one sentence; it is the structure of how state has been moved over time.

This does not solve multi-turn maintenance automatically. The large ontology needs some form of feedback about its own condition so that unsupported repetition and genuine correction produce different dynamics. The activity frontier can house this capacity within the same skeleton. Repetition that creates no new path distinction should not gain frontier authority solely through count. A genuine counterexample that changes the current path fan should reorganize the frontier. How training can acquire that distinction remains open.

### 8.3 Equal ease of functional invocation inside the frontier

The existence of a function and its ability to enter the activity frontier in the right situation are different properties. If architectural routing makes a function chronically inaccessible, even an excellent final decision rule cannot select it.

The activity frontier gives “equal ease of invocation” a concrete location. ## 9. Minimal Mathematical Translation: Local High-Gain Dynamics of the Activity Frontier

This section is not the principal result and does not claim a unique formalization. Its purpose is to test whether the preceding mechanism can be compressed into a mutually connected skeleton without falling back to an empty statement that “a generator function generates the context.”

### 9.1 Persistent state, input internalization, and the path fan

Let the persistent state of the selected design object at time $t$ be $B_t\in\mathcal B$. When external input $u_t$ arrives, it is internalized first:

$$
B_t^{+}=\mathcal U_\theta(B_t,u_t). \tag{1}
$$

Equation (1) specifies only that input changes the large ontology before it affects the frontier. It does not require $\mathcal U_\theta$ to be a separate module. Once internalized, the input is part of system state; its subsequent authority is determined by current effect rather than permanently by external origin.

Over an observation horizon $h$, represent the current path fan by a conditional path distribution:

$$
\Pi_t
=\mathbb P_\theta\!\left(L_{t:t+h}\in\cdot\mid B_t^{+}\right). \tag{2}
$$

$\Pi_t$ is an analytical coordinate. The system need not explicitly compute a complete distribution. Its substrate may maintain local samples, a continuous potential field, competing states, or another structure homologous to this bundle of possible continuations.

### 9.2 The frontier effect of an influence pattern

Let $m_i$ be a candidate influence pattern. Its effect on the current path fan can be characterized by comparing path distributions when the pattern is allowed to participate and when it is locally blocked. For a path feature $z$, define

$$
\nu_{i,t}(z)
=\log p_{i,t}^{\mathrm{on}}(z)
-\log p_{i,t}^{\mathrm{off}}(z). \tag{3}
$$

Here, $p_{i,t}^{\mathrm{on}}$ and $p_{i,t}^{\mathrm{off}}$ are the probabilities of the path feature when $m_i$ is respectively enabled or locally blocked; zero probabilities may be smoothed by the same small positive constant. If $\nu_{i,t}$ sharply separates routes with materially different consequences, $m_i$ has high current frontier value. If it affects all live routes similarly, one further question remains: would blocking it admit unacceptable routes from outside the current fan? If so, it maintains the feasibility boundary and may still need frontier support. Only when the common effect is stably carried by the large-ontology background, so that removing the explicit frontier pattern changes neither boundary nor live-path differences, may the pattern withdraw. Equation (3) is a counterfactual coordinate for evaluation, not an assumption that the running system performs exhaustive itemwise ablations.

Let $a_i(t)$ denote the instantaneous gain of pattern $m_i$ in the activity frontier. The frontier can be represented as a functional composition:

$$
C_t\equiv\sum_i a_i(t)m_i,
\qquad
a_i(t)\text{ remains high for only a small set of current patterns}. \tag{4}
$$

The sparsity in Equation (4) may arise from explicit capacity, competitive inhibition, low-rank structure, or another constraint. It does not require context to be a discrete set of content items.

### 9.3 Reauthorization dynamics

Let $q_t=Q(L_{t-r:t})$ be an analytical representation of the local trajectory formed so far. A candidate gain dynamics is

$$
\tau_i\dot a_i
=-\lambda_i a_i
+\alpha_i D_i(B_t,\Pi_t)
+\beta_i K_i(q_t,B_t)
-\sum_{j\ne i}\omega_{ij}a_j. \tag{5}
$$

Its terms have the following roles:

- $-\lambda_i a_i$ prevents prior frontier membership from renewing itself forever;
- $D_i$ represents present demand for the pattern’s boundary-preserving or path-distinguishing effect, as characterized by the relative path changes in Equation (3) together with whether ablation reopens unacceptable paths;
- $K_i$ represents the coupling between the current local trajectory and the conditions under which the pattern becomes accessible;
- the competitive term prevents all patterns from acquiring high gain simultaneously.

Equation (5) is not a proposed neural equation. It expresses four relations that must coexist: continuity decays, the current branch renews support for some influences, the local trajectory opens new historical routes, and capacity competition forces old influences to withdraw. Architecture and training must determine the actual form of each term.

The large ontology advances under frontier influence:

$$
\dot B_t
=F_0(B_t)
+\sum_i a_i(t)F_i(B_t). \tag{6}
$$

$F_0$ is the background dynamics in the absence of current high-gain patterns, and $F_i$ is the local contribution of $m_i$ to the next state. Equation (5) changes Equation (6); the new state generated by Equation (6) then changes Equations (2) and (5). Frontier and large ontology therefore form a loop.

The essential condition of reauthorization can be written counterfactually as

$$
a_i(t+\Delta)
=\mathcal A_i\!\left(
B_{t+\Delta},
\Pi_{t+\Delta},
q_{t+\Delta};
a_i(t)
\right),
\qquad
\mathcal A_i\not\equiv a_i(t). \tag{7}
$$

The earlier value $a_i(t)$ may support continuity, but it cannot determine the next value by itself. If a supposedly dynamic system remains close to $a_i(t+\Delta)=a_i(t)$ regardless of changes in state, it is copying the old frontier.

### 9.4 A minimal representation of causal traces

Represent causal trace $j$ as

$$
R_j=(\sigma_j,\delta_j,\chi_j,w_j), \tag{8}
$$

where $\sigma_j$ is its entry structure, $\delta_j$ its directional correction, $\chi_j$ its release condition, and $w_j$ its current strength and credibility. A coarse measure of trace action at time $t$ is

$$
g_j(t)
=w_j\,
\operatorname{Match}(q_t,\sigma_j)
\left[
1-\operatorname{Release}(q_t,\chi_j)
\right]. \tag{9}
$$

The term $g_j(t)\delta_j$ does not directly supply an answer. It enters the coupling in Equation (5) or the local dynamics in Equation (6), changing which patterns enter the frontier, which paths are delayed, and which distinctions reopen. Partial matching may produce a low-strength $g_j$; once a decisive difference appears, $\operatorname{Release}$ may rapidly remove the trace’s authority.

Equations (8) and (9) are the most exposed commitments in this monograph. They turn “timely déjà vu” into a historical deformation with an entry, a direction, and an exit. If future work shows that traces cannot be decomposed this way, the activity frontier may survive, but the specific construction of online path recognition offered here must be rewritten.

## 10. Four Load-Bearing Attacks Derived Directly from the Mechanism

Only experiments capable of striking the trunk are retained here. They should first be implemented in automata, rule-evolution systems, abstract control environments, or other nonlinguistic settings so that semantic association cannot impersonate the proposed mechanism.

### Test A: Does the frontier actually move with the internal trajectory?

Construct tasks that share an identical opening and reveal their decisive branch only after several internal transitions. Give three systems the same history, capacity, and total compute budget:

1. context is fixed once at task onset;
2. intermediate results may only be appended;
3. persistent state may reauthorize, withdraw, and admit influences as the new path fan develops.

The key observation is not whether the third system emits a different summary. It is whether, in the absence of new external input, a historical influence irrelevant at onset first acquires high gain after the branch appears; whether an initially important but later obsolete influence withdraws; and whether those changes alter the next transition.

**Direct failure condition:** after strict budget control, freezing the frontier and permitting reauthorization produce no stable difference; or the alleged frontier changes on the surface but its causal effect does not track the branch.

### Test B: Can identical surface content lose and regain authority rather than persist through copying?

Design a multi-step task with one constraint whose surface form never changes. Make the constraint decisive in the early phase, irrelevant in the middle, and decisive again after a later branch. Compare mechanical retention, mechanical deletion, and activity-frontier dynamics.

The frontier system should maintain high gain in the first phase, reduce the constraint’s authority in the middle, and restore it later without the constraint being re-entered externally. Even if the internal carrier displays the same sentence throughout, intervention must show that its causal authority fell and rose again.

**Direct failure condition:** the system can regulate influence only through textual presence or absence; or identical content, once retained, always keeps the same authority, while deleted content can never be regenerated by a later state.

### Test C: Can a causal trace transfer across surface relabeling and release at a decisive difference?

First expose the system to a nonlinguistic error in which “permission to enter candidacy” is treated as “final selection frequency,” followed by correction. Then apply two kinds of complete relabeling:

- **Isomorphic task:** replace every state name, action name, and surface code while preserving the error-generating relation and correction.
- **Near counterexample:** preserve most of the prefix but add a rule that explicitly binds candidate permission to final frequency.

In the isomorphic task, the trace should reopen the two-level distinction before the erroneous commitment. In the near counterexample, the trace may activate initially but should release when the binding rule appears. Transfer without release is overgeneralization; release without transfer is amnesia.

**Direct failure condition:** transfer disappears under surface relabeling; or the trace continues to impose the old correction after the decisive edge has changed.

### Test D: Can history change conduct before the old content is restored?

Make detailed recovery of the old case costly and place the commitment deadline before full recovery can finish. Compare three policies: always retrieve the full case, ignore history entirely, and allow a causal trace to exert a low-cost directional effect before expanding the old content on demand.

The relevant measurement is not the system’s report that the situation “feels familiar.” It is the first time the trace changes candidacy, delays commitment, or reopens a distinction. Targeted ablation of the trace route should increase the probability that the same error crosses the deadline without changing global knowledge.

**Direct failure condition:** all historical influence appears only after complete content recovery; or the alleged early directional effect cannot be distinguished by intervention from an ordinary conservative heuristic.

These four attacks strike the load-bearing claims directly. If A or B fails, the activity frontier may be rolling context under a new name. If C fails, the causal trace cannot support cross-content déjà vu. If D fails, traces may still serve as cues for explicit retrieval, but the stronger claim—that memory can act before appearing as content—must be abandoned.

## 11. Limitations and Open Problems

**L1 (Primary risk: the activity frontier may be only an attractive renaming).** If intervention cannot distinguish “the current state reauthorized the same influence” from “the system copied old content forward,” the activity frontier adds no mechanistic content. Test B bears this risk directly.

**L2 (The path fan may not be observable).** Equation (2) represents live near-term continuations as a path distribution, but a real system may maintain only a few implicit local tendencies. Measuring fan change without enumerating the future remains unresolved.

**L3 (Influence-pattern decomposition is non-unique).** The same local effect may be represented as one integrated pattern or several interacting terms. Equations (3)–(6) provide observational coordinates, not a canonical decomposition. A bad cut can manufacture spurious “necessary influences.”

**L4 (Frontier value remains dependent on goal and horizon).** Which influence distinguishes important paths depends on current objective, consequence, risk, and observation boundary. The model does not supply a universal cross-situational scoring function. It only localizes the comparison to the current branch.

**L5 (The reauthorization dynamics has no training algorithm).** Equation (5) states that decay, present demand, trajectory coupling, and competition must operate together. It does not show how those terms can be learned stably, how credit should be assigned, or what data would be required. The monograph remains a theoretical feasibility exploration.

**L6 (The three-part causal trace may not be the true minimal structure).** Entry, direction, and release are an AI-generated functional decomposition. A learnable mechanism may require more components, or the components may not be cleanly separable. The decomposition must not be reported as a user requirement or an established fact.

**L7 (“Changing conduct first” may be mimicked by a generic heuristic).** A system that becomes cautious near an old error is not necessarily using historical traces; it may have learned broad conservatism. Structural transfer, targeted ablation, and release behavior must jointly discriminate the mechanisms. Behavioral resemblance alone is insufficient.

**L8 (Release conditions may be harder to learn than entry structures).** Positive examples reinforce resemblance, while the decisive differences that invalidate an analogy are often rare. Without near counterexamples, a trace can easily grow into a stereotype.

**L9 (A dynamic frontier may oscillate).** Repeated reauthorization can produce switching, noise chasing, commitment failure, or high access cost. Allowing “unresolved” to become a valid frontier state may reduce premature commitment, but does not supply a stable stopping rule.

**L10 (Old traces may become obsolete or toxic).** A correction that was once useful may inhibit adaptation after an environmental change. Traces need to be weakened, revised, or supplied with new release conditions by current outcomes. This monograph does not show that such second-order plasticity can be stable.

**L11 (The boundary and sufficiency of the large ontology remain unresolved).** The design objective determines what belongs within the large ontology, but a chosen $B_t$ may omit variables that continue to affect the future. The path distribution is then not conditionally sufficient on $B_t$; the formalization must expand the state or admit non-Markovian dependence.

**L12 (Language can create the illusion of mechanism).** A model may explain “entry structure, directional correction, and release condition” correctly without those relations having altered its activity before the explanation was generated. Core evidence must come from temporal intervention and nonlinguistic tasks.

**L13 (Consciousness is only a functional classification here).** The activity frontier is called a consciousness interface because it occupies a low-capacity, high-gain niche that governs the next transition. No claim about subjective experience follows.

**L14 (Engineering implementation is intentionally deferred).** This monograph selects neither Transformer, recurrent network, Hopfield system, mixture of experts, graph diffusion, nor external controller, and it estimates neither compute nor latency. Existing tools gain a role only when they realize the causal relations specified here; they must not determine the theory’s shape in advance.

## 12. Conclusion

The Activity Frontier Model addresses three joined demands: context should be regenerated dynamically; historical influence should appear while redirection remains possible; and a current local trajectory should be able to reopen an older path. Its answer is one concrete candidate that runs from beginning to end.

The core is not a collection of modules but a moving working surface. At the locus where current activity still branches, a persistent large ontology raises a small set of genuinely path-altering influences to consciousness-level gain. After each transition, old influences cannot renew themselves through inertia, and new distinctions need not wait for another external input to appear. Context is reauthorized by the new state at every step. The same content can therefore be regenerated, while different words can remain a static copy.

Causal traces bring the past into this surface. Earlier errors and corrections need not lie somewhere as complete permanent records. They may alter the transition geometry of the large ontology. When the current local trajectory develops a similar causal organization, a trace first brings a distinction, caution, or alternative search route into the frontier; only then does the system decide whether reconstructing the old case is worthwhile. To keep familiarity from becoming identity, a trace must not only enter and redirect. It must release when a decisive difference appears.

One complete activity therefore has the form

$$
\text{persistent state}
\rightarrow\text{input internalization}
\rightarrow\text{path fan}
\rightarrow\text{activity frontier}
\rightarrow\text{transition}
\rightarrow\text{unresolved pressure / trace coupling}
\rightarrow\text{frontier reorganization}
\rightarrow\text{state writeback}.
$$

This chain is the actual trunk of the monograph. The nine equations, four experiments, and fourteen limitations merely translate it and leave exposed points at which it can break.

The likeliest failures are already visible. The activity frontier may be a literary restatement of recurrent attention with no independently identifiable causal property. Causal traces may fail to learn a structure that both transfers across content and releases accurately. The highest-value next step is therefore not another evaluation metric. It is to attack those two points: causally separate frontier reauthorization from ordinary copying, then place trace entry, correction, and release inside the same minimal nonlinguistic tasks and test whether they change the path before the second error is committed.

If that cannot be done, this construction should be dismantled. If only half of it works, preserve the working half. Its value does not depend on looking like a complete theory. The trunk is explicit enough to be cut, grafted, and extended with precision.
